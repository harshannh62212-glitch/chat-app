Left panel — Problem, requirements & sizing

1. Mission problem

Lunar surface operations require autonomous, hazard-tolerant transport of cargo and science payloads between the 40-ft logistics module and EVA work sites—eliminating astronaut manual haul overhead.

2. Design constraints & environmental drivers

Parameter Design value Impact
Gravity (Moon vs Earth) 1.622 m/s² (⅙ g) vs 9.81 m/s² Sized for Earth (78.5 N) to guarantee ~6× margin on Moon (13 N)
Gross vehicle mass 8.0 kg (2.85 kg dry + 5.0 kg payload) Structural truss & actuator load baseline

Center panel — Architecture, trade study & subsystems

1. Selected architecture: 6WD tracked skid-steer

  • Chassis: Modular PETG space-frame truss (4 perimeters, 35–40% gyroid infill) with brass heat-set inserts (M3/M4 installed at 230°C).

Trade study weights: slope 25%, sensors 20%, torque 15%, dust/power 15%, fab 15%, fit 10%

  • 6WD tracked skid-steer (94/100 — SELECTED): Best flotation on loose regolith, stable sensor horizon, compact cargo envelope.
  • 4WD wheeled skid-steer (65/100): Low complexity; high sinkage/slip on loose simulant.
  • Rocker-bogie (65/100): Superior obstacle climb; exceeds mass budget and classroom fabrication schedule.

2. Drivetrain & torque sizing (Earth demo @ 25°, rsprocket = 45 mm)

  • Required pull force: ≈ 80 N total (18.4 kg·cm stall torque per motor; ≥ 29.4 kg·cm at 1.6× safety factor).
  • Selected actuator: Dual JGB37-550 12 V planetary motors (30:1 / 50:1), 35 kg·cm stall, 160–200 RPM (0.7–0.9 m/s), 6 mm D-shaft with quadrature encoders.

3. Hardware evolution (dropped vs kept)

Subsystem Dropped baseline Upgraded selection
Motor driver L298N (saturation / thermal drop) Cytron MDD10A (10 A cont. / 30 A peak)
5 V power rail LM2596 3 A buck (Pi brownout on LiDAR spin-up) Dual isolated XL4015 (5 A / 8 A peak @ 5.1 V) or Pololu D24V50F5
Gearbox & structure 1:10 nylon spur (stripped teeth) & brittle PLA JGB37-550 metal planetary + PETG tracks/sprockets
Compute split Single-board bottleneck Arduino UNO R4 WiFi (hard real-time PWM) + Raspberry Pi (vision/nav)

4. Power & autonomy stack

  • Power bus: 12.8 V 4S LiFePO₄ (3–6 Ah, 1.5–2.5 hr runtime). 5 V budget 3.5 A nominal / 4.75 A peak (Pi 3.5 A + YDLIDAR 1.0 A + cam 0.25 A), protected by motor-bus TVS and opto-isolated PWM.
  • Navigation: YDLIDAR occupancy grid + A* + Pure Pursuit; optional ONNX neural pilot; JSON dodge assist (none | left | right); proximity auto-reverse supervisor.
  • Mission control telemetry: aegisv1.vercel.app

Right panel — Verification, test campaign & closed NCRs

1. Iterative engineering progression

  1. Phase 1 (Mockup): Cardboard prototype — wheelbase, CG, track vs wheel geometry.
  2. Phase 2 (CAD & fab): Fusion 360 truss, sprockets, sensor mast in PETG.
  3. Phase 3 (Digital twin): Physics sim — 25° slope climb, track slip, crater/pit guard.
  4. Phase 4 (Bench & FMEA): High-load PWM stress and NCR closure.
  5. Phase 5 (CDR → FDR): Full hardware-in-the-loop surface cargo run.

2. Simulation & hardware bench verification

  • Digital twin (run_nav_scenarios.py): Zero-collision pit avoidance (exit 0), CARGO RUN autonomous pallet docking, CSV telemetry via extract_sim_metrics.py.
  • Physical bench: Unloaded PWM sweep — zero driver faults, clean sensor streams.
  • 25° loaded incline crawl drawing < 12 A aggregate current.
  • 150 consecutive hard-stall cycles with zero MCU brownouts.
